Is College Still Worth It?
Since 1960, college has transformed from an opportunity for a minority of high school graduates into a near-default pathway for millions. Enrollment rates have climbed steadily, especially among women, while tuition has risen to unprecedented levels. As higher education expanded, so did its cost and its risks. Not every student finishes, and not every degree yields the same financial return. This interactive article explores six decades of data to examine whether college remains the most reliable pathway to economic opportunity, and for whom that promise holds true.
1. College Enrollment Over Time
Enrollment increased from ~45% to ~62% overall. Female enrollment rose dramatically, making college the dominant post-high school path.
2. Geographic Distribution
Total fall enrollment in degree-granting postsecondary institutions, by state: 1970–2022. Enrollment distribution varies across states, and growth is uneven geographically.
3. Equity in Access
Percentage of recent high school completers enrolled in college, by race/ethnicity: 1960–2022. Access differs by demographic group, with gaps narrowing or widening over time.
4. Accelerating Costs
Average undergraduate tuition, fees, room, and board rates: 1963–2023. Tuition growth significantly accelerated, with private institutions increasing the most.
5. Financing the Future
First-time students receiving financial aid and average amount awarded: 2000–2022. College increasingly depends on financial assistance as costs rise.
6. The Risk of Non-Completion
Graduation rate within 150% of normal time: 2000–2019. Not everyone finishes, and graduation rates vary by demographics and institution type. This visualization focus specifically on undergraduate education at 2-year institutions (such as community colleges, vocational schools, and technical colleges). It measures the "Graduation Rate within 150% of normal time." For a 2-year program, this means students who finished their degree or certificate within 3 years. Note: Data collection on Asian students only began in 2008.
7. Return on Investment
Recent Graduates Dataset (FiveThirtyEight). Earnings vary dramatically by major, and some degrees carry higher financial risk.
